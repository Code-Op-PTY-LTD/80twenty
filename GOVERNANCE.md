# Open Development and Governance

## Principles

80Twenty is developed in public. Anyone may use, study, modify and share the
Apache-2.0 source, open an issue, propose an Accord change or submit a pull
request. Acceptance is based on evidence and review rather than employment,
token ownership or permission from a participant-data custodian.

Raw participant data is not an open-source artefact. It must not be uploaded to
issues, pull requests, test fixtures, telemetry or training evidence.

## Contributions

Contributors retain their copyright and certify the Developer Certificate of
Origin with a signed-off commit. The project does not require copyright
assignment. Automated and AI-assisted work must be reviewed by the person who
submits it, who remains responsible for licensing, correctness and tests.

Pull requests require passing public checks and review appropriate to their
risk. Changes affecting file access, consent, update privacy, payments,
automatic execution or release qualification require two independent reviews,
including one security or privacy reviewer.

## Accord changes

Material protocol changes use an Accord Improvement Proposal. The proposal
states motivation, wire and privacy impact, compatibility, migration,
conformance tests, security considerations and alternatives. Discussion and
the final rationale remain public.

Emergency releases may address an actively exploited vulnerability, but must
publish the minimum safe explanation immediately and a complete retrospective
after disclosure becomes safe.

## Releases

Only reproducible, signed builds from reviewed source are official Companion
or Pulse releases. A merged pull request never authorises immediate execution
on participant machines. Genesis weights are released only with provenance,
evaluation and privacy records. The `80/20-qualified` label additionally
requires the Rolling Frontier Standard.

## Maintainers and forks

Maintainers publish their roles and material conflicts of interest. They may
reject changes for correctness, security, privacy, legal, scope or maintenance
reasons, with a public rationale. Apache-2.0 preserves the community's ability
to fork if governance fails.
