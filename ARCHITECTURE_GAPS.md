# Architecture Gaps and Build Order

KIN Seed 0 is a protocol genesis bundle, not yet a deployable network or newly trained LLM. These are the remaining layers.

## Priority 0 Truthful bootstrap

- Completed in Gate 0a: save and test locally generated implementations from two different seed models.
- Prove that no generated source or executable leaves either machine.
- Completed in Gate 0a: publish a deterministic specification-bundle digest.
- Add an independent conformance runner or cross-implementation comparison.

Gate 0a established cross-model behavioural compatibility, but not the no-code-transmission invariant. The next bootstrap build must be fully local, prevent the orchestrator from observing generated source, and have an independent process verify denied network access and the published fingerprints.

## Priority 1 First network-native learning checkpoint

- Select a laptop-trainable Apache-2.0 base model.
- Define a sparse or low-rank adapter-update format.
- Produce non-identical synthetic training shards.
- Replace shared HMAC secrets with public-key node identities.
- Validate and aggregate three or more independent updates.
- Reject a correctly signed malicious update.
- Publish a complete checkpoint provenance receipt.

## Priority 2 Transport and checkpoint consensus

- Content-addressed job and update transport.
- Peer discovery without one irreplaceable directory.
- Replicated validator committee and threshold checkpoint signatures.
- Dropout, replay, stale-job and partition handling.
- Model and adapter distribution with resumable integrity checks.

## Priority 3 Privacy and consent

- Capability-specific, reversible consent receipts.
- Secure aggregation so no coordinator sees an individual update.
- Update clipping and measurable privacy accounting.
- Memorisation, reconstruction and canary leakage testing.
- Sandboxed generated clients with reproducible permission observations.
- Third-party and bystander rights for emails, images and documents.

## Priority 4 Human participation integrity

- Privacy-preserving proof of one eligible participant.
- Device limits and diminishing returns under one identity.
- Cluster detection using several weak signals rather than latency alone.
- Separation between human contributor jobs and disclosed institutional compute.
- False-positive review and appeals.

## Priority 5 Compensation

- Funded price quotes and versioned formulas.
- Work, validation and settlement receipt chain.
- Contribution-value experiments and duplicate detection.
- Non-custodial test settlement, then regional fiat pilot.
- Reconciliation, disputes, tax records and payout-provider boundaries.

## Priority 6 Governance and legal operations

- Immutable privacy and openness commitments.
- Non-transferable contributor representation.
- Regional operators rather than one global employer or custodian.
- Protocol upgrade, fork and emergency-response rules.
- Model, dataset and participant-contribution provenance licences.
- Specialist review of privacy, employment, securities, tax, sanctions, exchange-control and financial-services exposure.

## Priority 7 Product and operations

- Human-readable installer and permission experience.
- Resource, battery, bandwidth, thermal and electricity limits.
- Monitoring that does not become participant surveillance.
- Support, incident response, vulnerability disclosure and signed security releases.
- Sustainable revenue from useful private, regional and specialist models.

## The critical path

The next credible milestone is not millions of users or a frontier model. It is three separately generated nodes on separately administered machines producing one demonstrably improved small-model adapter, with one malicious update rejected and every contribution recorded in a verifiable receipt chain.
